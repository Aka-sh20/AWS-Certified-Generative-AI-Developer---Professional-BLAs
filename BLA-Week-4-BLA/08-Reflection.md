# Reflection

These three presentations helped me understand Generative AI in a more practical way.

The first topic that took me some time was chunking. At first, standard, hierarchical, and semantic chunking sounded very similar. After comparing the examples, I understood that standard chunking mainly follows size, hierarchical chunking gives both small and larger context, and semantic chunking tries to keep related meaning together.

I also learned that RAG is not automatically good just because it retrieves information. The retrieved information still has to be relevant and useful. That is why metadata, evaluation, and data freshness are important.

The second video helped me understand the difference between just using a model and building a safer application around the model. Bedrock Guardrails, contextual grounding, and token-level redaction showed me that extra checks are needed in real applications.

Prompt Management and Bedrock Flows were also useful because they showed how prompts and different AI steps can be organized instead of writing everything separately.

The third video helped me connect Generative AI with data engineering. At first, Bedrock Data Automation, Data Wrangler, Glue, Transcribe, and Comprehend felt like many separate services. After looking at the workflow, I understood that each service handles a different type of data problem.

Overall, I learned that a good Generative AI application needs good data, good retrieval, safety controls, monitoring, and evaluation. The model is important, but the complete architecture is what makes the application useful.
